layer 1 of 6: the glass

How does it know where I touched?

The glass feels your finger and tells the app exactly where it landed. The app then has a few milliseconds to answer, so it feels instant. Making that answer smooth, clear and easy for everyone to use is where my work starts.

layer 2 of 6: the pixels

Who draws the buttons?

Code does. I write a short description of the screen, and when something changes, only that part gets redrawn. The same description can draw the app on Android and on iPhone.

  • Jetpack Compose
  • Flutter
  • Kotlin Multiplatform
  • Design systems
  • Wear OS

layer 3 of 6: the brain

How does it do many things at once?

When you open an app it needs your profile, your tickets and the map, all at once. Instead of fetching them one after another, the brain sends helpers out together, so you wait less. Keeping that brain tidy is what lets a team keep adding features without breaking old ones.

  • Coroutines & Flow
  • ViewModel
  • MVVM / MVI
  • Clean Architecture
  • Hilt & Koin
  • Modularization

layer 4 of 6: the memory

What happens when the internet goes away?

Most apps freeze. Mine write everything down on the phone first, then quietly send it when the signal comes back. Bus conductors print tickets all day like this, even on roads with no network.

  • Offline-first
  • Room & SQLite
  • WorkManager
  • Sync & conflict handling

    how I work

    Rules I build by

    • No signal is normal. I design for bad networks first, because that's where real people use apps: on buses, in fields, in basements.
    • Don't wait, unblock. If the backend isn't ready, I mock it and keep shipping.
    • Go and see. I've worked on site with enterprise clients like Nippon and Easybox to find the real problem before writing code.
    • Build it once. A shared component beats ten copies of the same button.
    • Measure, then fix. Crash reports and funnels tell me where to look. Guessing doesn't.
    • Leave it better. Every codebase I join gets cleaner architecture, more modules and more tests than it had.
